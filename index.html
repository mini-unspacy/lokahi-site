Welcome to Lokahi Outrigger Canoe Club

Hui Lokahi O Ke Kai Outrigger Canoe Club

Outrigger paddling has a deep tradition in Long Beach and across Southern California, and we’re here to keep that tradition alive on the water. Whether you’ve been paddling for years or have never picked up a paddle, come join us. We welcome paddlers of every age and ability, with programs for foundational paddling, competitive racing, and keiki (kids). All you need to bring is the desire to learn and the ability to swim — we’ll take care of the rest.

*New paddlers are welcome to join us for up to 3 practices before becoming a dues-paying member.

About Lokahi

The Lokahi History

Hui Lokahi O Ke Kai was founded in 1998 and is a member of the Southern California Outrigger Racing Association (SCORA). We’re a 501(c)(3) non-profit dedicated to perpetuating Hawaiian and Polynesian culture, honoring the aloha spirit, and building community through the sport of outrigger canoeing.

Our diversity is something we’re proud of — and it’s not just words on a website. Our members come from many countries and every walk of life: all ages, singles and couples, lifelong athletes and complete beginners. There’s a seat for novice paddlers, recreational paddlers, and competitive racers alike. Come see for yourself.

You’ll find our canoes and shed at Mother’s Beach on Naples Island in Long Beach, California. From those calm waters we launch our fleet of va’a (canoes) and connect with each other, with the ocean, and with the wider outrigger community.

Spirit of Aloha

We do our best to embody the Spirit of Aloha. The word itself means “the presence of breath” or “the breath of life” — from alo (presence, front, face) and ha (breath). Aloha is a way of living and of treating one another with love and respect. It begins with loving ourselves, and from there it flows outward to everyone we meet.

The old kahunas (priests) taught that living the Spirit of Aloha was a path to self-realization for body and soul. Aloha is energy — given and received. Aloha is harmony. The positive feelings it creates don’t disappear; they linger in the space around us, multiply, and reach others.

“Akahai”  Kindness, to be expressed with tenderness;
“Lokahi”  Unity, to be expressed with harmony;
“Oluolu”  Agreeable, to be expressed with pleasantness;
“Haahaa”  Humility, to be expressed with modesty;
“Ahonui”  Patience, to be expressed with perseverance.

Race Schedule

Racing isn’t required to be a Lokahi member, but we hope you’ll give it a shot — it’s a character-building challenge, and we think you’ll love it. Races run on Saturday mornings in place of our usual endurance practice. As we like to say: “a race isn’t done until the canoes are back at Mother’s Beach and unloaded by the club.”

First-time racers (and anyone needing a refresher) should check out our race cheat sheet.

  • Thursday before the race – we meet at Mother’s Beach to derig the canoes and load them onto the trailer along with any gear needed for race day. Many hands make light work.
  • Saturday (race day) – arrive at the race site by 5:45am to unload and rig the canoes. Food and drink are pot-luck — members bring pop-tents, mini grills, ice chests, and warm smiles. Racers stay until the canoes are loaded back on the trailer after Lokahi’s final race of the day.
  • Sunday (day after race) – we meet back at Mother’s Beach to unload, rig the canoes, and stow the club gear.

Join and Paddle with Us

Sign-Up Process

Membership Fees: OUTRIGGER MEMBERSHIP — $250 • KEIKI MEMBERSHIP — $150

Our season runs from mid-February through mid-December. We offer two membership options: Outrigger Membership and Keiki Membership. The Outrigger Membership is for adult competitive and recreational paddlers; the Keiki Membership is for paddlers nineteen and under. Outrigger members who want to race pay a small per-race fee for SCORA races (9-man race fees are billed separately). Non‑SCORA events may carry their own fees and costs.

How to pay your dues: After your first 3 practices, you can send your membership dues by Zelle to lokahi.treasurer@gmail.com. Please include your full name in the Zelle memo so we can match the payment to your registration.

What you get with a Lokahi membership:

  • More than 90 days of coached paddling (practice and racing) — that’s 90 days of being at the beach and on the ocean. Your co-workers will be jealous.
  • Real gains in strength, stamina, and endurance
  • Education on the sport, its history, on-water etiquette, and paddling technique
  • Access to club paddles, PFDs, and OC6s (six-person canoes)
  • Travel to other Southern California beaches for races throughout the season
  • Potlucks and team events — trust us, these are a blast: relaxation, laughter, and tall tales
  • A real role in carrying forward the tradition and culture of outrigger canoeing

Prepping for Practice

How to prep for your first practice:

  1. Fill out the form above.
  2. Wear workout clothes that can get completely wet.
  3. Bring a reusable water bottle or hydration pack — no glass or metal please. (We’re committed to protecting the beach and ocean, so we keep single-use plastics off the sand.)
  4. Leave purses and large backpacks at home if you can. We have a locked shed for phones, keys, and footwear during practice. (We paddle barefoot.)
  5. Bring your own outrigger paddle if you have one. If not, no problem — we have club paddles you can use at no charge. It’s actually a great way to figure out the right size before buying your own.

How to Plan for Your 1st Outrigger Canoe Race

We spend a lot of time prepping for our first race: fitness, stroke, communication, fuel strategy, etc. But what about the actual race day? Fret not. We’ve got you covered.

Keep These Tips Top of Mind

  • You have stepped into an activity that is 2,000+ years old. We work hard and show respect to each other on the beach, during practices, and even in the heat of racing for top position near the finish line.
  • Nothing new on race day. Anything you wear, use, or consume should be something you’ve repeatedly tested under race-like conditions (aka: during hard, hot practices). By not testing and refining prior to race day you not only risk your individual performance, but also put your crew at a disadvantage.

“Nothing New on Race Day” — Common (and Very Real) Examples

ChoiceResult
“I got a new hydration pack yesterday from REI and I’m going to use it for the first time on race day.” Backpack – straps rub upper pecs and inner biceps raw. Hip hydration pack – sat on & pinched the hose and couldn’t get water out for the entire race.
“I have never tried this sports drink powder. I’m going to roll the dice and use it on race day.” Stomach got super upset after trying the new mix and didn’t drink anything in the race.
“I got these sweet new shorts that make me look super rad.” Turns out the seam runs across the butt cheek and it was rubbed super raw. That prevented them from committing to a clean and strong stroke.
“Paddle felt super heavy today, so I grabbed a shorter, lighter one from the rack.” Paddle was too short, couldn’t get a clean catch, and threw off timing for the whole crew.

The Days Leading Up to the Race

Wednesday

  1. Don’t go super hard during practice. You need to be super fresh for your Saturday race.
  2. Don’t leave your race stuff in the locker … take it home.

Thursday

  1. Canoe and Gear Loading – Team joins together at the beach to get the canoes and related gear loaded onto the trailer and truck. No paddling. Just come in clothes that are comfy and allow you to lift and transport stuff. Thanks to Tony & Denise for all the logistics around getting the gear to the race site. BIG help.
  2. Prehydrate – Eat water-rich raw fruit and veg: watermelon, cantaloupe, cucumbers, tomatoes, and lots of water. Nothing worse than letting your crew down because you’ve been “hydrating” on lattes, margaritas, and sodas.
  3. Your Gear – What do you need in the actual canoe? It’s different for everybody. Whatever that is for you, it needs to be secured to your body (other than a hand water bottle, of course … if you use those).

    Why? If you huli you don’t want to make your life and the ocean’s life miserable because you’ve had a yard sale all over the ocean: your hat is floating that way, sunglasses fell off and sank to the ocean floor, sports gels floating the other way, your visor sank also, etc. It’ll be complex enough with your paddles floating, doing the headcount, and the canoe being upside down and possible swell rolling things around if you race the long course. Don’t make it worse for your crew.

  4. Shirts – Do you have a team jersey? If not, wear a red top if you have one.
  5. Contribution to the Tent – What healthy food or drinks will you contribute to the team tent for race day?
  6. Utensils – What non-single-use-ocean-friendly items do you need to bring for yourself? (Reusable cup with your name on it, reusable plate/bowl, bandana as a napkin, etc.) We don’t do paper plates, plastic forks, or paper napkins.
  7. Warm Layers – What warm layers will you bring for the early AM pre-race time frame and the possible windy post-race time frame? (Hoodie? Change of clothes? etc.)
  8. Beach Chair – Put your name on it. People always leave a few each race.

Friday

  1. More Prehydration – Stay on top of it.
  2. Mental Visualization – Think about how good your stroke is. Think about how well rested and hydrated you are. Think about how much value your seat will bring to the crew on race day because you’re good-to-go. Think about the crowded race start and that you’re expecting to be super close to other canoes and around choppy, stirred-up water … and that’s OK and the norm. Think about your respect for the ocean, its wildlife, fellow racers, and the paddlers that came before us.
  3. Rest – Go to bed SUPER early. Binge-watch that new sci-fi show on Sunday.

Race Day (Saturday)

5:45 AM

  1. Car parked at the race site at the latest.
  2. The lots are big beach lots, but everybody is arriving from all of the other teams at the same time.
  3. Leave all of your race gear in the car. You’ll learn where the team tent is after canoe unloading.
  4. Just wear your slippers or shoes.

6 AM

  1. Canoe Unloading – Meet at the canoe unloading area to help your teammates unload and rig-up the canoes. Just look around for all of the trucks and massive trailers. Generally speaking, the loading zones are at the far left or right of the venue. San Diego races generally have their zones at the far left.
  2. The aim is to get the gear off the trailer and out of the truck quickly and SAFELY. That way we can get the trailer/truck moved so other teams can unload too.

7 AM

  1. You’ll learn where the tent is located and go to your car to get your gear.
  2. Drop your gear off and learn where you can lend a hand (setting up tables, finishing tent prep, etc.).

Rest of the Day

  1. It’s a full day commitment.
  2. You’re going to have a lot of down time before and after your race. You’ll be able to firm-up friendships with teammates, walk around and purchase gear, buy food from vendors, chat with other clubs, nap, snack on food in the tent, read, tell tall tales, etc. Whatever you do, add to the overall vibe … be nice, be open, smile, grab litter if you see it, lend a hand if somebody from another club needs it, say hi to our neighboring club when you see them (Kahakai) … whatever it is you do, just make it positive.
  3. Even if you are not a steersperson, plan on attending the Steersman Meeting. They’ll announce it on the beach before each division. You need to be in the know. It’s safer for everybody if everybody knows the course layout and details for that day.
  4. Each time one of our crews goes down to the beach to get ready to head out for their race, we ALL go down to the water with them. We help with the canoes and paddles, give high-fives to all of the other crews on the beach (yes, even the other clubs), and cheer them on as they head out. This is outrigger culture.

End of the Day

  1. As soon as the last crew finishes, we’ll all meet at the loading zone & trailers again for de-rigging and loading of all the canoes and gear.
  2. This is an important part of the race day. Many hands make for light work.
  3. If the loading zone is on grass, bring your slippers with you because some race sites have grass that has stickers that will make your feet very sad. (Ouch!!)

Sunday

8:45 AM

  1. Don’t be late.
  2. Meet at Mother’s Beach to unload trailers and canoes.
